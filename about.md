## Short bio

Mahdi Zareei is a Research Professor at the School of Engineering and Sciences,
Tecnológico de Monterrey, Mexico, and a Senior Member of the IEEE. His research
sits at the intersection of information security, applied machine learning, and
natural language processing, building on a strong foundation in wireless sensor
and cognitive radio networks. He is a member of Mexico's National System of
Researchers (SNI, Level I) and serves as an Associate Editor for IEEE Access,
PLOS One, and the Ad Hoc & Sensor Wireless Networks journal.

## Full biography

Mahdi Zareei is a Research Professor in the School of Engineering and Sciences at
Tecnológico de Monterrey, Mexico. He received his B.Eng. in Software Engineering
from Payame Noor University, Iran, and his M.Sc. in Computer Science from the
University of Science, Malaysia (USM) in 2011. In 2016 he completed his Ph.D. with
the Communication Systems and Networks Research Group at the Malaysia-Japan
International Institute of Technology, Universiti Teknologi Malaysia, where his
dissertation addressed mobility-aware medium access control for cognitive radio
sensor networks. During his doctoral studies he was a visiting research student in
the Sampei Laboratory at Osaka University, Japan.

He joined Tecnológico de Monterrey in 2017 as a Postdoctoral Fellow and has served
as a Research Professor there since 2019. His early work focused on wireless sensor
networks, cognitive radio, MAC and routing protocols, and energy-harvesting
networks for environmental monitoring. His current research centers on information
and network security, applied machine learning, and natural language processing —
including authentication and access-control schemes, trust models for vehicular and
fog computing, deep learning for biomedical and image analysis, and large-scale
sentiment and emotion analysis of social-media data.

Dr. Zareei is a Senior Member of the IEEE and a member of Mexico's National System
of Researchers (SNI, Level I). He serves as an Associate Editor for IEEE Access,
PLOS One, and the Ad Hoc & Sensor Wireless Networks journal, and reviews regularly
for leading venues including ACM Computing Surveys and the IEEE Internet of Things
Journal. He has authored more than one hundred peer-reviewed publications and
actively supervises graduate students across his research areas.
