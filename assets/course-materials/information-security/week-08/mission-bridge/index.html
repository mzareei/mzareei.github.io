Field report

This happened for real

Two incidents show what breaks when the parts of a secure channel fail: the trust chain, and the library underneath it.

DigiNotar (2011)

Attackers breached the Dutch certificate authority DigiNotar and issued fraudulent certificates for domains including *.google.com. Those forged certificates were used to intercept and monitor Gmail traffic for internet users in Iran, undetected for weeks.

Lesson: certificate trust depends on the entire chain, not just a site's own configuration — one compromised CA can undermine every certificate it ever issued. Browsers eventually revoked all trust in DigiNotar, and the company was declared bankrupt within months.

Heartbleed (2014)

A memory-handling bug in OpenSSL, the TLS library running a large share of the web's HTTPS servers, let attackers repeatedly pull small chunks of server memory — sometimes including private keys, session cookies, and passwords — with no trace left in normal logs.

Lesson: a flaw in the software implementing the channel breaks every guarantee built on top of it. A perfectly issued, perfectly trusted certificate cannot protect you if the encryption library underneath is broken.
