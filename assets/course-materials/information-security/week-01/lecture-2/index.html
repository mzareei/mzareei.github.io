How do ethics and law differ?

◆ What do you think?

Is this “unauthorized access”?

Real case

hiQ Labs v. LinkedIn — is scraping illegal?

hiQ scraped public LinkedIn profiles for analytics tools (Keeper, Skill Mapper). LinkedIn responded with cease-and-desist letters.

YearWhat happened
2019Accessing public data ≠ “unauthorized access” under the U.S. CFAA. Blocking access seen as anti-competitive.
2021The case was returned for reconsideration.
2022Original ruling reaffirmed: public scraping remains outside “unauthorized access” under the CFAA.
Late 2022But there was breach of contract (scraping + fake accounts). Settlement: hiQ pays $500K and ceases operating on LinkedIn data.

02

The legal framework in Mexico

Three laws every cybersecurity professional should know.

Overview

Three pillars of the Mexican framework

©️

Copyright (LFDA)

Protects intellectual property — including digital works — against unauthorized reproduction or distribution.

🛒

E-commerce law

Sets standards for secure online transactions and protects consumers’ data.

🛡️

Personal-data protection (LFPDPPP)

Requires private entities to protect the personal data they hold.

Ley Federal de Derechos de Autor

LFDA: copyright, applied to software

“The Ley Federal de Derechos de Autor protects the rights of authors and creators against unauthorized reproduction or distribution, including digital works.”

In software & cybersecurity it covers: copyright protection, authorship, software licensing, online infringement, and penalties.

LFDA · interesting facts

What the LFDA means for security people

🔓

Art. 114 — security testing

Circumventing technological protection measures is not a violation when done for certain purposes — like security testing, or accessibility for people with disabilities.

🌐

Art. 114 — ISPs

Limits ISP liability for infringement on their networks — if they remove infringing content upon notice.

🏷️

Art. 232 — copyright metadata

Prohibits tampering with copyright-management info (e.g., author metadata). Altering it can hide the source of pirated content.

LFDA · penalties

Infringement costs — measured in multiples

ArticleFine (multiples of daily minimum wage)
Art. 2308,000–22,000 for serious violations (unauthorized reproduction/distribution); 1,500–8,000 for lesser ones.
Art. 2315,000–40,000 for commercial infringement (reproduction & distribution for profit).
Art. 2321,000–20,000 for tampering with copyright-management information.
Art. 233Up to 50% higher for large-scale infringers (e.g., publishers and broadcasters).

Note: in Mexico such fines are now calculated in UMA (Unidad de Medida y Actualización), not minimum wage — verify current values.

LFDA · no prison?

Fines under the LFDA, but prison under the Criminal Code

The LFDA: fines only

The LFDA establishes only monetary fines for copyright infringement — not prison sentences.

The Criminal Code: prison

Art. 424: unauthorized reproduction/publication of a work → 6 months to 6 years. Making/distributing/selling devices to decode encrypted satellite signals → 3 to 10 years.

In practice

Prison mainly applies in severe cases: large-scale piracy for profit.

Ley de Comercio Electrónico

E-commerce: protecting the online consumer

Regulates online commerce, ensuring secure electronic transactions and protecting consumers’ data.
  • The regulation began in April 1998, driven by the OECD.
  • In Mexico, the Federal Consumer Protection Law promotes and protects consumer rights, seeking fairness and legal certainty between suppliers and consumers.
  • The Mexican cyber-consumer is protected when buying online — overseen by Profeco (the Federal Consumer Protection Agency).
Personal-data protection

LFPDPPP: rights over your personal data

Principles & rights

Sets principles and rights for personal data held by private parties: consent, notice, access, rectification, cancellation, and opposition.

Oversight: INAI

Creates an independent body (INAI) to oversee compliance, hear complaints, and impose sanctions.

Verify

Mexico’s regulatory institutions have changed in recent years — confirm the current data-protection authority before class.

LFPDPPP · definitions

Personal data vs. sensitive data

Personal data

Any information concerning an identified or identifiable natural person.

Sensitive data

Data affecting a person’s most intimate sphere, or whose misuse could cause discrimination or grave risk.

Examples

Sensitive data includes: health, genetic data, religion, philosophical or political views, and sexual orientation.

LFPDPPP · sensitive data

Sensitive data demands extra protection

  • Extra protections are mandated for sensitive data.
  • It is prohibited to build databases of sensitive data without justifying explicit, legitimate purposes tied to the entity’s activity — limiting unnecessary collection.
  • There are specific security requirements: administrative, technical, and physical safeguards appropriate to the risk and sensitivity of the data.
LFPDPPP · compliance

Breach notice, ARCO rights, and penalties

📣

Notify the breach

Breaches that significantly affect data subjects must be reported to them so they can take defensive measures.

🙋

ARCO rights

Individuals can exercise Access, Rectification, Cancellation, and Opposition over their data.

⚠️

Penalties

Fines, prison in cases of bad faith or fraud, and doubled penalties for violations involving sensitive data.

Quick check

Which law applies?

Match

(1) An online store leaks customers’ purchase histories. (2) Someone resells licensed software by copying it. (3) An unauthorized charge on an online purchase. — Which law is primary in each case?

For discussion

(1) Data protection (LFPDPPP) · (2) Copyright (LFDA) · (3) E-commerce / consumer protection. Several can overlap — that’s the point.

Ownership & the “right to be forgotten”

Who decides what is yours?

◆ One last question

The “right to be forgotten”

In 2014 the EU Court of Justice ruled that citizens have a “right to be forgotten”: Google must not return links to information that is “inaccurate, inadequate, irrelevant or excessive.” Which one should Google NOT return?

⚖️

A

A story about a criminal conviction that was revoked on appeal.

🩺

B

A doctor asks to remove links to news stories about botched procedures he performed.

For discussion

A. A revoked conviction is now inaccurate/irrelevant → should not be returned. The doctor’s information is of public interest and usually stays. “Forgetting” balances privacy against the public’s right to know.

04

Privacy

What it is, what threatens it, and how to protect it.

Before we continue…

What is privacy?

A person’s ability to control how data about them can be collected, used, and shared by someone else.
Key

The key word is control — not “secrecy.” Privacy is deciding who knows what about you.

Old worry, new scale

Privacy isn’t a new problem — but the scale is

People have always worried about what others — friends, enemies, governments — might know about what they do.

What changed

The scale and magnitude at which information about us can be collected — and the ways it can be used, shared, or sold.

Draw the line

What’s private… and what isn’t?

Usually private

  • Finances: statements, cards, banking records
  • Health/medical conditions; biometrics (fingerprints)
  • Legal matters; political beliefs
  • Web-browsing habits; communications (emails, calls)
  • Past history (right to be forgotten)

Not private? (discuss)

  • Where I live? My citizenship?
  • That I’m registered to vote?
  • My salary? (… really?)
Note

The line depends on context and culture — hence all the question marks.

Not only individuals

Privacy matters for organizations too

Universities, hospitals, and charities require privacy — they must protect the data of the people they serve or employ.

Think

A leak at a hospital or university harms thousands of people who never chose to take that risk.

Threats

How privacy gets eroded

📱

Social media

🍪

📍

Sometimes we opt in

Store loyalty cards: we trade data for discounts.

Key takeaways

Legal & ethical, in one slide

  • Ethics is an individual standard; law is societal and enforced — they often reinforce each other.
  • In Mexico: LFDA (copyright), e-commerce / Profeco, and LFPDPPP (personal data) form the core framework.
  • Data ownership is philosophically slippery — which is why regulation matters.
  • Privacy is control, not secrecy: who collects, uses, and shares your data.
